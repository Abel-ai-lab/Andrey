This card measures the three methods 0.12.0 makes supported, Inter-IAMB, MMHC, and RSMAX2, against
bnlearn on the graph each returns. Each result holds for the measured conditions; it depends on the
data, settings, and hardware.

::::
:::::

Inter-IAMB, MMHC, and RSMAX2 are supported methods. We compare each with bnlearn 5.2.1 on the
graph it returns, scored against that graph's own truth: the CPDAG for MMHC and RSMAX2 and for
Inter-IAMB's default `structure="cpdag"`, the skeleton for `structure="skeleton"`, and the moral
graph for `structure="moral"`, which bnlearn's `learn.mb` blankets joined by the AND rule give.

A setting is one size, sample size, and mean degree. D is Andrey's error minus bnlearn's on the
same dataset. A setting with at least 10 paired datasets is worse when the paired 95% bootstrap
interval of the mean D lies wholly above 0, better when wholly below, and a tie otherwise. The
time ratio is bnlearn's fit time over Andrey's, the median of the settings' median ratios. In no
setting does bnlearn make significantly fewer errors.

## Method

**Data.** Linear-Gaussian data from Erdos-Renyi graphs, standardized, with 20, 50, and 100
variables, 1,000 and 5,000 rows, and mean degree 2 and 4. Each setting has up to 20 datasets, one
per seed, with the columns in a shuffled order; every setting keeps at least 10 paired datasets.

**Fits.** One fit at a time on one core, Fisher-z tests at alpha 0.05 (bnlearn `test="zf"`) and the
Gaussian BIC for the score phase (bnlearn `score="bic-g"`), a 600 s cap per fit, and one untimed
warm-up fit except at 5,000 rows, mean degree 4, and 50 or more variables, where there is none. A
fit that reaches the cap is a timeout. It is counted as coverage, left out of the pairs, and never
scored as an empty graph. The Andrey fits all finished; the timeouts are bnlearn's.

**Errors.** The structural Hamming distance of the returned graph to its own truth. The tables give
the median over the paired datasets, for Andrey, bnlearn, and the empty graph; D is the mean of the
per-dataset differences, the statistic of the verdict. A DAG that bnlearn returns is compared
through its CPDAG.

## Other changes in 0.12.0

The calibration changes of 0.12.0 are measured in the docstrings of the code they change, on the
[API pages](../api.md), and in the [changelog](../changelog.md), not on this card: the
`normal_scores` test, the calibrated G-square and chi-square tests, the Markov check
(`andrey.markov_check`), and the bootstrap's subsampling and averaged graph. The two sections below
give DirectLiNGAM's new order measures and the false discovery rate control of PCMCI and Granger.
The [upgrade guide](../guides/upgrading.md) shows the renamed and removed arguments.

### DirectLiNGAM's order measures

DirectLiNGAM's `measure` sets how the order search picks the most exogenous remaining variable:
the default `"pwling"`, or `"skew"` and `"tanh"`, which cost far less. We measured them on
simulated linear data with unit-variance noise from Erdos-Renyi graphs of mean degree 2, with 10
and 20 variables and 500 and 2,000 rows, 10 datasets in each of 4 cells per noise, on standardized
columns. The mean errors (SHD) of `"pwling"` were 0.7 to 0.9 for every noise, and no cell had fewer
errors with `"skew"` or `"tanh"` by a paired 95% interval.

- **Exponential noise**, skewed with either sign: `"skew"` matched `"pwling"` (0.6) and `"tanh"`
  did worse (4.7).
- **Laplace noise**: `"tanh"` matched it (0.8) and `"skew"` did worse (16.2).
- **Student t noise** with 5 degrees of freedom, symmetric and heavy-tailed: `"tanh"` had 1.4 and
  `"skew"` 9.8.
- **Uniform noise**, symmetric and sub-Gaussian: both were worse than `"pwling"`'s 0.8. `"tanh"`,
  which assumes sparse (super-Gaussian) noise, had 35.1 and put 17% of the true edges in the right
  order, and `"skew"`, which needs skewed noise, had 22.4 and 48%, against 99% for `"pwling"`.

The order search alone took 0.1 to 0.3 of `"pwling"`'s time, and the whole fit about half to two
thirds of it, as the pruning step does not change.

### FDR control in PCMCI and Granger

PCMCI and Granger correct their p-values for the number of lagged links tested by default
(`fdr_method="fdr_bh"`). We compared the correction with `fdr_method="none"` on 20 simulated
linear-Gaussian VAR series per cell, with 2 expected parents of each variable at each lag and no
same-step links, from 5 to 80 variables, with 500 and 1,000 time steps, and `tau_max` of 1 and 2
(20 cells); Granger's series were standardized. The errors are the mean missing plus extra lagged
links, compared by the paired 95% bootstrap interval of the difference.

- **Cells.** With `"fdr_bh"`, PCMCI had fewer errors than with `"none"` in 16 cells, the same in
  3, and more in 1: 12.5 against 11.7 with 5 variables, 500 steps, and `tau_max=2`. Granger had
  fewer in 15, the same in 4, and more in 1: 11.9 against 10.9 in the same cell. From 20 variables
  on, both were better in every cell.
- **The empty graph.** With `"none"`, the mean errors were above the empty graph's in 5 of the 20
  cells, for both methods; with `"fdr_bh"`, in none. At 80 variables, 1,000 steps, and
  `tau_max=2`, PCMCI with `"none"` had 779 errors, above the empty graph's 481, with 616 extra
  links and 163 missed true links on average; with `"fdr_bh"` it had 252, with 13 extra and 239
  missed. Granger had 768 errors with `"none"`, with 603 extra and 165 missed, and 256 with
  `"fdr_bh"`, with 13 extra and 243 missed. On 10 series of 160 variables with 1,000 steps and
  `tau_max=2`, Granger's median errors were 2,855 with `"none"`, above the empty graph's 973, and
  546 with `"fdr_bh"`.
- **Small graphs.** `"fdr_bh"` trades missed true links for far fewer extra ones: with 5 to 10
  variables the two differ by at most 2.8 errors for PCMCI and 2.7 for Granger. With 5 variables
  and `tau_max=2`, `"fdr_bh"` removed PCMCI's extra links (1.2 to 0.4 at 500 steps) and missed more
  true ones (10.4 to 12.1); for Granger, 1.1 to 0.4 and 9.8 to 11.5.

## Limits

The bnlearn comparisons cover 20 to 100 variables and 1,000 to 5,000 rows on linear-Gaussian data
with Fisher-z tests. Larger graphs, other data types, and other tests are not measured. bnlearn
reached the 600 s cap in some settings at 5,000 rows and mean degree 4, so those settings have fewer
paired datasets; each table lists the datasets it pairs. At 20 variables and mean degree 4, MMHC and
RSMAX2 make 28.5 to 32 errors against 40 for the empty graph, the closest of any setting.

## Builds and versions

Every fit ran on build `31f418d` on AMD EPYC 7713 and 9534 and Intel Xeon Gold 6542Y processors.
Later commits change `src/` only in method status, docstrings, the text of the argument concepts,
and the bootstrap's averaged-graph fallback; none changes the output of a method fit, checked bit
for bit on every method. The bootstrap's averaged graph changes only where its fallback would have
