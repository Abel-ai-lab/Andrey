## The benchmarks

The earlier release cards keep the numbers of the builds they measured. This release's summary adds
the comparison of DAGMA with its authors' package, with a check of DAGMA and NOTEARS on measured
data ([DAGMA](#dagma), [NOTEARS](#notears)).

## DAGMA

`andrey.dagma` (Bello et al., 2022) fits a weighted DAG by minimizing least squares with an `l1`
penalty under a log-determinant acyclicity constraint. It is supported, with a gap: its result
depends on the data's scale. The [API reference](../api.md#supported-methods) describes it.

We compared it with its authors' package on the DAGMA paper's simulation: linear models with weights
from +-[0.5, 2], unit noise variances, and 1,000 rows, on Erdos-Renyi graphs with `2 d` edges and
scale-free graphs with `4 d`, 10, 20, 50, and 100 variables, Gaussian, exponential, and Gumbel
noise, and 20 datasets per setting. It made no more errors than the package in any of the 24
settings with every column z-scored and any of the 18 on the simulation's own units (raw columns),
and in 17 of the 18 settings where each variable is divided by its standard deviation as it is
generated (rescaled); the other one was worse. On raw columns it made fewer errors than the empty
graph in all 18 settings. On z-scored columns it made fewer in none of the 24, and more in 16, as
the package did. On raw columns the variance of a variable grows along the causal order, which the
least-squares fit reads (varsortability; Reisach et al., 2021), and z-scoring removes it. The
[comparison page][nd] has every setting.

### On measured data

The Sachs data hold 853 observational measurements of 11 proteins and phospholipids with a 17-edge
consensus network (`andrey.load_dataset("sachs")`), and 7,466 measurements in all nine experimental
conditions, which the methods here read as one sample. The table gives the errors between the
estimated CPDAG and the consensus network's, the empty graph's being the 17 edges. The first number
is the fit on all the rows, and the second, in parentheses, the mean over 10 resamples of the rows.
DAGMA and NOTEARS draw no random numbers; `pc` and `ges` run at their defaults. On the raw 853
measurements the consensus network's varsortability is 0.67, and 0.50 once the columns are z-scored.

| Method | 853 cells, raw | 853 cells, z-scored | 7,466 cells, raw | 7,466 cells, z-scored |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Empty graph | 17 | 17 | 17 | 17 |
| `dagma` | 13 (14.2) | 13 (12.1) | 12 (12.1) | 13 (12.6) |
| `dagma(robust=True)` | 13 (12.5) | 13 (12.6) | 13 (13.1) | 15 (15.8) |
| `notears` | 14 (16.0) | 13 (12.7) | 21 (22.6) | 13 (13.0) |
| `notears(robust=True)` | 14 (14.3) | 13 (12.6) | 18 (18.0) | 18 (16.7) |
| `pc` | 11 (14.5) | 11 (14.5) | 29 (27.7) | 29 (27.7) |
| `ges` | 11 (14.4) | 11 (14.4) | 36 (36.7) | 36 (36.7) |

On the 853 observational measurements the four fits made fewer errors than the empty graph, on raw
and z-scored columns alike, and on all the rows `pc` and `ges` made fewer than them. On the pooled
measurements, which mix experiments, `dagma` stayed below the empty graph, `notears` and
`notears(robust=True)` did not on the raw columns, and `pc` and `ges` made more errors than the
empty graph. The fits are on one dataset with a consensus network, so the table says little about
other data. The release's [lane file][lane] records every fit's errors under `sachs`.

### The likelihood path

`robust=True` follows the first fit with the likelihood path of Deng et al. (2024). Its likelihood
no longer includes the log-determinant of `I - W`, which is zero for every DAG: the gradient of the
term is not zero while a fit is cyclic, and the authors' code leaves it out. We ran it on the
simulation of their paper, linear Gaussian models with weights from +-[0.5, 1.5], noise standard
deviations from [0.1, 0.7], 1,000 rows, columns shuffled, and 20 datasets per setting, and scored
the CPDAG errors, the paper's measure, on z-scored columns, against the path run with the authors'
code.

| Setting | Empty graph | `dagma(robust=True)` before | `dagma(robust=True)` now | The authors' code |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Erdos-Renyi, 10 variables | 20.00 | 23.05 | 21.30 | 20.05 |
| Erdos-Renyi, 20 variables | 40.00 | 44.25 | 39.95 | 42.40 |
| Scale-free, 10 variables | 17.00 | 23.50 | 17.90 | 18.15 |
| Scale-free, 20 variables | 37.00 | 54.05 | 39.05 | 39.35 |

On z-scored columns `robust=True` does not change the finding: in none of the four settings does it
make fewer errors than the empty graph (the 95% interval of the difference includes 0 in each), and
neither does the path run with the authors' code. The [lane file][lane] records these means, with
their intervals, under `likelihood_path`.

## NOTEARS

`andrey.notears` (Zheng et al., 2018) is supported since 0.17.0, and its comparison with its
authors' code is on the [0.17.0 release card](0.17.0.md#notears). Its result also depends on the
data's scale (Reisach et al., 2021), and its `robust=True` path had the same log-determinant term;
the table under [DAGMA](#on-measured-data) gives its errors on the Sachs data. On the paper's
simulation of Deng et al. (2024) at 10 variables, with Erdos-Renyi graphs and 20 datasets, the CPDAG
errors of `notears(robust=True)` were 18.50 on z-scored columns, against 20.00 for the empty graph
and 18.45 for the path run with the authors' code, and 11.80 on raw columns, against 11.75 for the
authors' code; before the change they were 16.60 and 13.25.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey (andrey-core) in the harness's
environment. Times will differ with the hardware and build; the errors should match closely.

| Comparison | Lane | Sizes | Datasets x repeats | Wall cap (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| DAGMA against its authors' package | `notears_cpu.py`, judged by `notears_summary.py` | the DAGMA paper's simulation: Erdos-Renyi and scale-free graphs with 10, 20, and 50 variables on every scale, and 100 on z-scored columns | 20 x 1, and 20 fresh x 1 for a worse cell | 1,800 |

The fits on the Sachs data and the likelihood-path runs have no lane in the harness. The
[lane file][lane] records their data, settings, and the code the reference column ran.
