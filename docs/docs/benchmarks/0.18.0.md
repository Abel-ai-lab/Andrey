0.18.0 makes DAGMA, a continuous-optimization method, supported with its gap stated: its result
depends on the data's scale, and on z-scored columns neither it nor its authors' package made fewer
errors than the empty graph. Against that package on its paper's simulation it made no more errors
in any of 42 settings of raw and z-scored columns. Its `robust=True` path, and that of NOTEARS, now
follows the authors' code, and we checked both methods on measured protein data.

The release also adds three experimental methods, for functional causal modeling. DAS prunes SCORE's
causal order at scale, SkewD reads the direction of a pair whose noise is skewed, and CPCM reads it
where an effect follows a chosen distribution, such as counts. `load_dataset` loads the Tübingen
cause-effect pairs, ANM, PNL, and LOCI return the same direction under any order of the rows, and
the project states how it uses AI tools.

## The benchmarks

The earlier release cards keep the numbers of the builds they measured. This release's summary adds
the comparison of DAGMA with its authors' package, with a check of DAGMA and NOTEARS on measured
data ([DAGMA](#dagma), [NOTEARS](#notears)). DAS, SkewD, and CPCM are experimental and are
benchmarked when they are promoted ([Experimental methods](#experimental-methods)).

## DAGMA

`andrey.dagma` (Bello et al., 2022) fits a weighted DAG by minimizing least squares with an `l1`
penalty under a log-determinant acyclicity constraint. It is supported, with a gap: its result
depends on the data's scale. The [API reference](../api.md#supported-methods) describes it.

We compared it with its authors' package on the DAGMA paper's simulation: linear models with weights
from +-[0.5, 2], unit noise variances, and 1,000 rows, on Erdos-Renyi graphs with `2 d` edges and
scale-free graphs with `4 d`, 10, 20, 50, and 100 variables, Gaussian, exponential, and Gumbel
noise, and 20 datasets per setting. It made no more errors than the package in any of the 24
settings with every column z-scored and any of the 18 on the simulation's own units (raw columns),
and in 17 of the 18 settings where each variable is divided by its standard deviation as it is
generated (rescaled); the other one was worse on its first 20 datasets and tied on 20 fresh ones. On
raw columns it made fewer errors than the empty graph in all 18 settings. On z-scored columns it
made fewer in none of the 24, and more in 16, as the package did. On raw columns the variance of a
variable grows along the causal order, which the least-squares fit reads (varsortability; Reisach et
al., 2021), and z-scoring removes it. The [comparison page][nd] has every setting.

### On measured data

The Sachs data hold 853 observational measurements of 11 proteins and phospholipids with a 17-edge
consensus network (`andrey.load_dataset("sachs")`), and 7,466 measurements in all nine experimental
conditions, which the methods here read as one sample. The table gives the errors between the
estimated CPDAG and the consensus network's, the empty graph's being the 17 edges. The first number
is the fit on all the rows, and the second, in parentheses, the mean over 10 resamples of the rows.
DAGMA and NOTEARS draw no random numbers; `pc` and `ges` run at their defaults. On the raw 853
measurements the consensus network's varsortability is 0.67, and 0.50 once the columns are z-scored.

| Method | 853 cells, raw | 853 cells, z-scored | 7,466 cells, raw | 7,466 cells, z-scored |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Empty graph | 17 | 17 | 17 | 17 |
| `dagma` | 13 (14.2) | 13 (12.1) | 12 (12.1) | 13 (12.6) |
| `dagma(robust=True)` | 13 (12.5) | 13 (12.6) | 13 (13.1) | 15 (15.8) |
| `notears` | 14 (16.0) | 13 (12.7) | 21 (22.6) | 13 (13.0) |
| `notears(robust=True)` | 14 (14.3) | 13 (12.6) | 18 (18.0) | 18 (16.7) |
| `pc` | 11 (14.5) | 11 (14.5) | 29 (27.7) | 29 (27.7) |
| `ges` | 11 (14.4) | 11 (14.4) | 36 (36.7) | 36 (36.7) |

On the 853 observational measurements the four fits made fewer errors than the empty graph, on raw
and z-scored columns alike, and on all the rows `pc` and `ges` made fewer than them. On the pooled
measurements, which mix experiments, `dagma` stayed below the empty graph, `notears` on the raw
columns and `notears(robust=True)` on both did not, and `pc` and `ges` made more errors than the
empty graph. The fits are on one dataset with a consensus network, so the table says little about
other data. The release's [lane file][lane] records every fit's errors under `sachs`.

### The likelihood path

`robust=True` follows the first fit with the likelihood path of Deng et al. (2024). Its likelihood
no longer includes the log-determinant of `I - W`, which is zero for every DAG: the gradient of the
term is not zero while a fit is cyclic, and the authors' code leaves it out. We ran it on the
simulation of their paper, linear Gaussian models with weights from +-[0.5, 1.5], noise standard
deviations from [0.1, 0.7], 1,000 rows, columns shuffled, and 20 datasets per setting, and scored
the CPDAG errors, the paper's measure, on z-scored columns, against the path run with the authors'
code.

| Setting | Empty graph | `dagma(robust=True)` before | `dagma(robust=True)` now | The authors' code |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Erdos-Renyi, 10 variables | 20.00 | 23.05 | 21.30 | 20.05 |
| Erdos-Renyi, 20 variables | 40.00 | 44.25 | 39.95 | 42.40 |
| Scale-free, 10 variables | 17.00 | 23.50 | 17.90 | 18.15 |
| Scale-free, 20 variables | 37.00 | 54.05 | 39.05 | 39.35 |

On z-scored columns `robust=True` does not change the finding: in none of the four settings does it
make fewer errors than the empty graph (the 95% interval of the difference includes 0 in each), and
neither does the path run with the authors' code. The [lane file][lane] records these means, with
their intervals, under `likelihood_path`.

## NOTEARS

`andrey.notears` (Zheng et al., 2018) is supported since 0.17.0, and its comparison with its
authors' code is on the [0.17.0 release card](0.17.0.md#notears). Its result also depends on the
data's scale (Reisach et al., 2021), and its `robust=True` path had the same log-determinant term;
the table under [DAGMA](#on-measured-data) gives its errors on the Sachs data. On the paper's
simulation of Deng et al. (2024) at 10 variables, with Erdos-Renyi graphs and 20 datasets, the CPDAG
errors of `notears(robust=True)` were 18.50 on z-scored columns, against 20.00 for the empty graph
and 18.45 for the path run with the authors' code, and 11.80 on raw columns, against 11.75 for the
authors' code; before the change they were 16.60 and 13.25.

## Experimental methods

Each of these methods follows its paper and is tested on fixed cases and on simulated data with a
known graph. None is benchmarked yet: a campaign against its authors' code comes before it is
supported, and its docstring's **Limitations** paragraph, on the [API pages](../api.md), says what
is known so far. The [functional causal models post](../../blog/20261103-andrey-0-13-0.md) describes
DAS, SkewD-MV, and the CPCM graph search, and the [pairwise direction
post](../../blog/20261015-andrey-0-4-0.md) the two pair tests.

- **DAS** (`andrey.das`; Montagna et al., 2023) finds SCORE's causal order of nonlinear data with
  additive Gaussian noise, then screens each variable's candidate parents on the same estimate of
  the score's Jacobian, at most 20 by default, and prunes only those with CAM's tests. It is for
  graphs with too many variables for SCORE's pruning.
- **SkewD** (`andrey.skewd`) and **SkewD-MV** (`andrey.skewd_mv`; Klippert and Marx, 2026) read the
  direction of a pair, and choose a DAG of a few variables in PC's equivalence class, under
  location-scale noise whose distribution is skew-normal. With symmetric noise, the model reduces to
  LOCI's.
- **CPCM** (`andrey.cpcm`) and its graph search (`andrey.cpcm_graph`; Bodik and Chavez-Demoulin,
  2025) assume that each variable given its causes follows a family you choose, such as Gaussian
  with a changing mean and scale, Poisson, or Pareto, and read the direction from which model leaves
  residuals independent of the cause. They suit counts, positive or heavy-tailed variables, and
  pairs where each column has its own family.

## Other changes in 0.18.0

`andrey.data.load_dataset("tuebingen")` loads the Tübingen cause-effect pairs (Mooij et al., 2016),
108 pairs of real measurements, each with its reference direction and a weight for averaging over
the collection. Andrey downloads the archive from its original server and checks it, since the
collection states no license.

ANM, PNL, and LOCI fit each direction on its rows sorted by the data, so a result does not depend on
the order of the rows. Every pairwise method decides a tie between the two directions by the same
rule, and a tie in both the p-values and the statistics gives no edge. On the recorded-output and
recovery inputs, no graph changes.

With columns in very different units, LOCI's two directions' log p-values of a table that mirrors
itself can differ by up to about 6e-4, relative to the larger of 1 and their absolute values,
through the solver's stopping tolerance, 1e-6 as in LOCI's paper, so a nearly tied direction can
follow that noise.

The DirectLiNGAM order search stops scoring a candidate once it can be neither picked nor tied,
which makes DirectLiNGAM and its multi-group variant faster on larger graphs with the same output;
VARMA-LiNGAM and longitudinal LiNGAM use the same search, with the same output. The Fisher z test,
the normal-scores test, and two other p-values compute their upper tail directly, so a strong
dependence keeps a positive p-value down to about 1e-308. The normal-scores test reads data from one
environment only, and GIN keeps its latents' numbers under any column order. The examples gain a
notebook on missing values.

The project states how it uses AI tools: every change goes through the same checks and a pull
request that a person answers for, benchmark numbers come only from recorded runs, and every post
and release card, this one included, ends with a statement of how AI tools were used to write it.
The [AI policy](https://github.com/Abel-ai-lab/Andrey/blob/main/DEVELOPMENT.md#ai-tools) has the
rules.

The [changelog](../changelog.md) lists every change.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey (andrey-core) in the harness's
environment. Times will differ with the hardware and build; the errors should match closely.

| Comparison | Lane | Sizes | Datasets x repeats | Wall cap (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| DAGMA against its authors' package | `notears_cpu.py`, judged by `notears_summary.py` | the DAGMA paper's simulation: Erdos-Renyi and scale-free graphs with 10, 20, and 50 variables on every scale, and 100 on z-scored columns | 20 x 1, and 20 fresh x 1 for a worse cell | 1,800 |

The fits on the Sachs data and the likelihood-path runs have no lane in the harness. The
[lane file][lane] records their data, settings, and the code the reference column ran.
