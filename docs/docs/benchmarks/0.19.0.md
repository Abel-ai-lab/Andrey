0.19.0 makes DAS supported. On the simulation of its paper, with every column z-scored or each
variable rescaled, it made no more errors than the code published with the paper in any of 20
settings from 10 to 200 variables, and fewer on the first 20 datasets of each, in a tenth to a

units, where the variance grows along the causal order, that code made fewer errors on sparse
graphs; DAS reads standardized columns, so its result does not depend on the units.

It also makes CPCM supported, its pair test's forced direction and its graph search. On the pairs
and graphs of its paper, up to 8 variables, they made as few errors as the code published with the
paper, except the conservative estimate at $\gamma = 0$ under `"pareto"` (see [the Pareto
gap](#the-pareto-gap)), and fewer than a coin flip, the majority direction, or the empty graph,
except on Table 2's Pareto pairs drawn by the authors' own generator, where neither implementation
did (see [the PARg gap](#the-parg-gap)), with the graph search about ten times faster. The
conservative estimate under `"pareto"` and Table 2's Pareto setup stay out of the supported claim
until those gaps are fixed.

And it makes SkewD supported, its pair test and its graph version SkewD-MV. On the skewed pairs of
its paper, two Gaussian location-scale pair sets, and its graphs of 6 and 10 variables, they made as
few errors as the authors' recorded outputs and fewer than a coin flip or the empty graph, and
SkewD-MV fewer than PC, CAM, NoGAM, SCORE, or RESIT. SkewD's default criterion is now the
likelihood, which named all 160 pairs right. Both are slow: a pair takes 15 to 22 CPU minutes, and
a graph a median of 24 to 31.

## The benchmarks

The earlier release cards keep the numbers of the builds they measured. This release's summary adds
the comparison of DAS with the code published with its paper, and the lane file adds its errors
against the empty graph up to 1,000 variables and its errors and times beside `cam`, SCORE, and
NoGAM ([DAS](#das)). It also adds the comparison of CPCM's pair test and graph search with the code
published with their paper, with LOCI, ANM, and PNL on the same pairs and eight graph methods on
the same graphs ([CPCM](#cpcm)), and the comparison of SkewD and SkewD-MV with the authors'
recorded outputs, beside LOCI, ANM, PNL, and CAM on the same pairs and five graph methods on the
same graphs ([SkewD](#skewd)).

## DAS

`andrey.das` is DAS (Montagna et al., 2023). It finds SCORE's causal order (Rolland et al., 2022)
from the leaf up with a kernel estimate of the score, keeps for each variable the earlier variables
whose entries of the score's Jacobian are clearly above zero, at most 20 of them, and prunes only
those with the tests of CAM (Buhlmann et al., 2014). It returns a DAG and a causal order, and gives
the same result in any units and column order. It is supported. The [API
reference](../api.md#supported-methods) describes it.

We compared it with DAS in dodiscover, the code published with the paper, at the paper's settings,
on the simulation of the SCORE and DAS papers: Erdos-Renyi graphs with as many edges as variables
(ER1) or four times as many (ER4), each link one draw of a Gaussian process, Gaussian noise, and
1,000 rows, at 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 variables, 20 datasets per setting, with every column
z-scored and with each variable divided by its standard deviation before its children read it
(rescaled). DAS made fewer errors (SHD) than dodiscover in all 20 settings on their first 20
datasets, and more in none. The summary counts a setting as better only once fresh datasets repeat
it, and these were not rerun, so it records them as ties. DAS's median fit took a tenth to a
fiftieth of dodiscover's time, the gap growing with the variables. dodiscover's estimate reads the
columns' units, and z-scoring costs it errors: on the same 20 ER1 datasets of 50 variables it made
18.9 errors on the simulation's own units and 31.7 on z-scored columns.

The table shows z-scored columns. SHD is the mean number of edge errors over the 20 datasets, the
empty graph's SHD is the number of edges, and a fit's time is the median CPU seconds on one core.
For ER1 and for ER4 at 10 variables the datasets are seeds 0-19; for ER4 from 20 to 200 variables
they are seeds 20-39, since seeds 0-19 were used while the method was built.

### On the simulation's own units

On raw columns the variance of a variable grows along the causal order in this simulation, by a
varsortability of 0.8 to 0.9 on the ER1 graphs (Reisach et al., 2021). DAS standardizes the columns
inside its order search, so it returns the same graph on raw and z-scored columns, while dodiscover
reads the scales. We measured raw columns as context, not as a claim, at 10, 20, and 50 variables.
On ER4 graphs DAS made fewer errors or as many. On ER1 graphs dodiscover made fewer at 20 and 50
variables, and the gap repeated on 20 fresh datasets:

## CPCM

`andrey.cpcm` and `andrey.cpcm_graph` are CPCM, conditionally parametric causal models (Bodik and
Chavez-Demoulin, 2025). Each variable given its causes follows a family you choose, such as an
exponential law whose rate changes with the cause, a Poisson count, or a Gaussian whose mean and
scale change; each value mapped through its fitted distribution function is independent of the
causes only in the causal direction. `cpcm` decides the direction of a pair by an HSIC test in each
direction, and `cpcm_graph` returns the DAG whose residuals a joint independence test finds most
independent, with a penalty per edge. Both are supported, the pair test for its forced direction;
its conservative estimate under `"pareto"` is not (see [the Pareto gap](#the-pareto-gap)), nor is
it on Table 2's Pareto setup (see [the PARg gap](#the-parg-gap)). The [API
reference](../api.md#supported-methods) describes them.

We compared them with `CPCM_graph_estimate`, the code published with the paper (commit 2ada90e, with
R 4.6.1 and mgcv 1.9.5), on the paper's own settings, with the same family or the same family
collection in both: Table 1's exponential pairs, model (16)'s Pareto pairs, Table 2's benchmark
pairs, and the graphs of Section 6.4 and Appendix B.1.1. Where the paper lets the data pick the
family, Andrey gets, per column, the family the authors' support rule picks (a count is Poisson, a
positive skewed column exponential, a column of values of at least 1 with a power tail Pareto, and
any other the fixed-scale Gaussian). Andrey's `"pareto"` family takes values greater than 1 only,
since a 1 has an infinite deviance, so a column the rule assigns to Pareto must hold values strictly
above 1. Pairs are 100 per setting, graphs 20, and most settings were drawn again on fresh seeds,
pairs 100-199 and graphs 20-39; a blank fresh cell in the tables is a setting not fitted again. A
setting counts as better or worse only when the fresh seeds repeat it; the fresh graphs have no run
of the authors' code, so they check Andrey against the empty graph only.

#### The PARg gap

On PARg from the authors' generator Andrey named the true direction on 0.38 of the first seeds'
pairs and 0.47 of the fresh ones, and the authors' code on 0.38 and 0.46, no better than a coin
flip, so Table 2's PARg setup is out of the supported claim on both sources. The first cause we see
is the family: the support rule gives the effect the exponential family (on 167 of 200 pairs) or
the Pareto family of scale 1 (33), and neither holds a Pareto of scale $\theta$ and shape 1. The
authors' code with the paper's sequential choice of the family, which Andrey does not implement,
was right on 66 of the 99 first-seed pairs it fitted (one stopped with an error) and 66 of the 100
fresh ones (a check of the cause, not a measured arm). The
second is the data: $\theta$ is constant for causes below 0 and the effect's mean is infinite; no
other method was right on more than 0.44 of the pairs. Our next step is to implement the paper's
sequential choice, rerun PARg on both sources beside the authors' code with the same choice, and
judge it again.

### The conservative estimate

With `alpha=0.05`, CPCM draws an edge only when exactly one direction's residual tests independent,
and the paper's Section 6.2 reads its five outcomes on model (16) at 500 rows: with $\gamma > 0$ it
names X1 -> X2, with $\gamma = 0$ it finds both directions plausible, and with $\gamma < 0$ the
columns test independent (an empty graph). The table gives the share of pairs on the first seeds,
and the share of the claimed outcome on the fresh seeds.

| -2 | no orientation | 0.03 | 0.04 | 0.86 | 0.03 | 0.04 | 0.94 | 0.99 | 0.99 |

No orientation is any of the outcomes that draw no edge: empty, both, or neither. We fixed this
pooled outcome as the claim for $\gamma \le 0$ before the runs. On it, every $\gamma$ ties on the
first seeds, and at $\gamma = 0$ Andrey misses it more often on both seed sets, 6 and 8 more pairs
in 100, worse by the interval on the fresh seeds only (from 0.02 to 0.14 more misses). The pooled
measure counts Andrey's extra "neither" results as hits, though (4 against 1 on the first seeds),
and "neither" is the outcome the paper reads as the model's assumptions not holding. On "both"
alone, the paper's claim at $\gamma = 0$, Andrey is worse on both seed sets: 0.73 against 0.82 on
the first seeds (0.09 more misses, interval from 0.04 to 0.15) and 0.78 against 0.85 on the fresh
seeds (0.07, from 0.02 to 0.13). So the conservative estimate under `"pareto"` is not supported:
it stays out of the claim, with the cause and the fix below. At $\gamma = 2$, X1 -> X2 beats the
reverse by 0.87 (interval from 0.80 to 0.93).

#### The Pareto gap

On some pairs one of Andrey's two p-values falls to nearly 0 where the authors' code gives 0.05 to
0.85, while the other agrees with it to two or three digits. Of the 400 fits at $\gamma = 0$, 16 do
so, 14 of them on the tenth of fits whose cause has its largest value more than 844 times its
median; there one fit in three does, and elsewhere at most one in forty. The same tables hold most
of Andrey's wrong pairs at $\gamma = 2$ and 2,000 rows: 7 of its 8 have a column whose largest value
is more than 5,000 times its median. The likely cause is the rank-10 thin plate spline of the cause
on such extreme values, which Andrey builds itself; we have not yet traced how it differs from the
authors' (mgcv's). Our next step is to compare the two bases and fitted parameters on those pairs,
follow the authors' construction where they differ, rerun model (16), and judge the conservative
estimate again.

## SkewD

`andrey.skewd` is SkewD (Klippert and Marx, 2026). It fits the location-scale noise model
$y = f(x) + g(x)\,n$ with skew-normal noise $n$ in each direction of a pair, by a penalized
likelihood over B-splines of the proposed cause, and names the direction whose model has the larger
likelihood (SkewD-LL, `criterion="likelihood"`, the default) or whose scaled residual an HSIC test
finds more independent of the cause (SkewD-IT, `criterion="independence"`). Both criteria read the
same two fits. `andrey.skewd_mv`, SkewD-MV, learns PC's equivalence class and returns its DAG whose
models, one per variable given its parents, have the largest likelihood. Both are supported, for
pairs and for graphs of up to 10 variables. The [API reference](../api.md#supported-methods)
describes them.

We compared them with the authors' recorded outputs: the result for each dataset of the code
published with the paper (commit 312632b), run by its authors with the paper's arguments on the
data files we read. Each recorded pair keeps the standardized data it fitted, which equals the data
Andrey read on all 320 pairs of the eight sets, and the recorded outputs give the paper's Table 5
and Table 2. To check them, we ran that code again on 15 pairs and 5 graphs. Its optimizers are
stochastic, so the scores changed, but the likelihood criterion named the same direction on all 15
pairs and the independence criterion on 13, and all 5 graphs came out the same, from the same PC
class. Those graphs were the ones whose classes are cheapest to score. Pairs are 20 per set, graphs
20 per size and 20 more on fresh seeds, all with 1,000 rows. A setting counts as better or worse
only by the paired interval, and for graphs only when the fresh seeds repeat it.

Andrey's fits ran on a development build from before 0.18.0. Its SkewD code is this release's,
except that the release sums each column's mean and variance over its sorted values, so that they
do not depend on the row order, which changes the standardized values in their last digits; refits
of three pairs and one graph gave the same edges on both builds.

## Reproduce

You can find environment setup, campaign drivers, and scores in the [benchmark harness
guide][harness]. To rerun the comparison, install Andrey (andrey-core) in the harness's
environment. Times will differ with the hardware and build; the errors should match closely.

| Comparison | Lane | Sizes | Datasets x repeats | Wall cap (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| DAS against dodiscover | `stein_cpu.py` with `andrey.das.numpy` and `dodiscover.das` | ER1 and ER4 with 10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 variables, z-scored and rescaled, and up to 50 on raw columns; dodiscover also at ER4 with 500, z-scored | 20 x 1, 20 fresh x 1 for a worse cell, 5 x 1 at 500 | 1,800 to 100 variables, 3,600 at 200, 7,200 at 500 |
| DAS against the empty graph, and beside `cam`, SCORE, and NoGAM | `stein_cpu.py` with `andrey.das.numpy`, `andrey.cam.numpy`, `andrey.score_matching.numpy`, `andrey.nogam.numpy`, and `baseline.empty` | ER1 and ER4 with 10 to 1,000 variables, z-scored; SCORE up to 200 and 2 datasets at ER4 with 500, NoGAM at 10 and 20 | 20 x 1 | 1,800 to 100 variables, 3,600 above |

The no-standardization variant of the raw-column check is not part of the method or the harness.
The [lane file][lane] records its errors per set of datasets.

The CPCM comparison is not yet a lane of the harness. Its [lane file][lane-cpcm] records every
setting with its datasets, families, packages, and intervals: the authors' code at commit 2ada90e
with R 4.6.1, mgcv 1.9.5, dHSIC 2.2, gamlss 5.5.5, and bnlearn 5.2.1, one fit per core with one BLAS
thread, and a cap of 1,800 s per fit (3,600 s for the authors' code at 8 variables), which no fit
reached.

The SkewD comparison is not yet a lane of the harness either. Its [lane file][lane-skewd] records
every set with its datasets, the reference check, and the intervals: the authors' recorded outputs
of the code at commit 312632b, checked by a fresh run with NumPy 1.26.4, cmaes 0.13.1, and
scikit-optimize 0.10.2, up to 24 hours per pair; Andrey one fit per core with one BLAS thread, pairs
stopped at 2 CPU hours and graphs at 4, which no fit reached.
