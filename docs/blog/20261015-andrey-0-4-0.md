# Pairwise direction

Does `x` cause `y`, or does `y` cause `x`? With only two variables, independence tests cannot tell:
both directions fit the data equally well. ANM and PNL instead use the shape of the relationship
to infer direction. We have supported both methods since 0.4.0, and LOCI, for a pair whose noise
grows or shrinks with the cause, since 0.16.0. 0.18.0 adds two experimental methods, SkewD for
skewed noise and CPCM for an effect that follows a chosen distribution, loads the Tübingen
cause-effect pairs, and makes the directions of ANM, PNL, and LOCI independent of the order of the
rows.

We explain how two variables can reveal their direction, run ANM and PNL on two pairs, and report
the 0.4.0 benchmark results.

## Direction from two variables

PC and GES can orient an edge only through a third variable, such as a common effect. A pair has
no third variable, so `x -> y` and `y -> x` explain the data equally well. These methods leave the
edge undirected.

A model of the mechanism can break the tie. Suppose `y = f(x) + e`, where the noise `e` is
independent of `x`. Regress `y` on `x`, and the residual looks independent of `x`. Regress `x` on
`y`, and in general no residual is independent of `y`, unless `f` is linear and the noise is
Gaussian.

Each test fits both directions and checks whether the residual is independent of the proposed
cause. It uses HSIC, a kernel test that detects nonlinear dependence as well as correlation.
The direction whose residual looks independent is the causal one.

The methods fit different models:

- **ANM** (additive noise model) fits `y = f(x) + e` by Gaussian-process regression. The direction
  is generally identifiable when `f` is nonlinear or the noise is non-Gaussian.
- **PNL** (post-nonlinear model) fits `y = f2(f1(x) + e)`, where `f2` is invertible. This is an
  additive noise model observed through a distortion, such as a sensor that saturates. ANM is
  the case where `f2` changes nothing. PNL fits each transform as a sum of smooth basis functions.
- **LOCI** (location-scale noise model,
  [Immer et al., 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/immer23a.html)) fits
  `y = f(x) + g(x) * n`, where the noise scale `g > 0` changes with the cause. The spread of `y`
  can therefore grow or shrink with `x`. ANM's additive noise does not allow that. In each
  direction, LOCI fits a Gaussian whose mean and variance are spline functions of the proposed
  cause. It then tests the residual scaled by the fitted spread.
- **SkewD** (skew-normal location-scale model, Klippert and Marx, 2026), supported since 0.19.0,
  fits LOCI's model with noise that follows a skew-normal distribution, so the noise can be skewed,
  and by default names the direction whose model has the larger likelihood. With symmetric noise,
  its model is LOCI's.
- **CPCM** (conditionally parametric causal model, Bodik and Chavez-Demoulin, 2025), supported since
  0.19.0, models the effect given its cause with a family you choose, such as Gaussian, Poisson, or
  Pareto, whose parameters are smooth functions of the cause. It suits counts, positive or
  heavy-tailed effects, and pairs where each column has its own family.

## Supported methods

A method becomes supported once we benchmark it against another package and check its results on
known data. ANM and PNL became supported in 0.4.0; PNL was experimental before.

| Method | Model | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| [ANM](../docs/api/andrey.anm.rst) | `y = f(x) + e` | A two-node DAG, both p-values, and both test statistics |
| [PNL](../docs/api/andrey.pnl.rst) | `y = f2(f1(x) + e)` | A two-node DAG, both p-values, and both test statistics |
| [LOCI](../docs/api/andrey.loci.rst) | `y = f(x) + g(x) * n` | A two-node DAG, both p-values, both test statistics, and both log-likelihoods |

Choose a method for your data:

- **ANM** when the effect is a smooth function of the cause plus independent noise. Its regression
  time grows with the cube of the number of rows, so it suits up to a few thousand rows.
- **PNL** when the effect may pass through a distortion or you have more rows. In the 0.4.0
  benchmarks, its median fit took under 2 s at 5,000 rows.
- **LOCI** when the spread of the effect changes with the cause, such as a reading
  that becomes noisier as its value grows. With its default criterion, the HSIC test's time and
  memory grow with the square of the number of rows.

LOCI is new in 0.16.0. We compared it with its authors' code on 100 pairs in each of 26 settings.
Neither of its criteria made significantly more errors, and its median fit was 40 to 912 times
faster with the default criterion. The [0.16.0 release card](../docs/benchmarks/0.16.0.md#loci)
reports every measurement. Keep the default criterion unless you know the noise is Gaussian: the
likelihood criterion assumes it, and under Gumbel noise it chose the right direction for fewer than
half the pairs. LOCI decides one pair because its paper defines no procedure for more variables. For
a graph, use CAM. The cause must range without bounds, so a uniform cause is outside its model.

## SkewD and CPCM

SkewD and CPCM, available since 0.18.0, follow their papers and are tested on simulated pairs. Both
are supported since 0.19.0, whose release card compares CPCM with its paper's code and SkewD with
its authors' recorded outputs. SkewD favors accuracy on skewed noise and gives up time: on its
paper's skewed pairs, LOCI was as accurate on most sets in under a second, and SkewD more accurate
on the most skewed location-scale set, at a median of 15 to 22 CPU minutes per pair;
`andrey.config(n_jobs)` runs its searches on threads, which cuts the wall time. Use **SkewD** when
the effect's spread changes with the cause and its noise is skewed, and **CPCM** when the effect is
a count or has a known distribution. For more than two variables, use their graph versions,
`skewd_mv` and `cpcm_graph`, which the [functional causal models post](20261103-andrey-0-13-0.md)
describes.

All five methods assume that no hidden common cause links the pair. When one is plausible, use the
methods in the [0.2.0 post](20261012-andrey-0-2-0.md).

## Reading the result

Each test returns a two-node DAG. When it decides by independence tests, as ANM, PNL, and CPCM do
and LOCI does by default, its `metadata` gives both directions' results, `forward` for `x -> y` and
`backward` for `y -> x`:

- `metadata["p_values"]`: the p-values of the two independence tests. A large p-value means the
  residual looks independent of the proposed cause, so that direction fits.
- `metadata["statistics"]`: the two HSIC statistics, each divided by its mean under independence.
  Smaller values mean weaker dependence.

Without a significance level, the edge points in the direction with the larger p-value. On a large
sample, both p-values can fall far below 1e-16. The tests compute each p-value through its
logarithm to preserve precision. They compare the two logarithms, which keep their order even when
both p-values fall below about 1e-308 and print as 0. The statistics decide only between equal
p-values: the smaller one determines the direction. This rule selects a direction even for
unrelated variables.

Two numbers are equal here when they differ by at most `1e-9` times the larger of 1 and their
absolute values. When both the p-values and the statistics are equal, the method draws no edge.
Every pairwise method uses this rule. From 0.18.0, ANM, PNL, and LOCI also fit each direction on
its rows sorted by the data, so the result does not depend on the order of the rows.

With a significance level such as `alpha=0.05`, a direction fits when its p-value exceeds the
level. The test draws an edge only when exactly one direction fits.

You read LOCI's result the same way under its default, `criterion="independence"`: it tests each
direction's scaled residual, `(y - mu(x)) / sigma(x)`, against the proposed cause, and its p-values
decide as ANM's do. With `criterion="likelihood"`, it selects the direction whose model has the
larger mean log-likelihood and draws no edge on a tie. `metadata["log_likelihoods"]` gives the mean
log-likelihoods under both criteria. SkewD reads the same way, with the same two criteria, except
that its default is the likelihood: a bare `skewd` call returns `metadata["log_likelihoods"]`, with
no p-values or statistics, and selects the direction with the larger mean log-likelihood. CPCM
decides by its two residual tests, as ANM does.

## Benchmark results

For 0.4.0, we compared both methods with causal-learn, a Python package that implements them, on
simulated cause-effect pairs. We tested ANM on additive-noise pairs and PNL on post-nonlinear pairs.
The precise p-values of 0.16.0 changed no direction on 970 pairs drawn as these benchmarks draw
theirs, from 200 to 16,000 rows.

| Method | Compared with | Andrey's speedup | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANM | causal-learn | about 2x | the same as causal-learn's on every pair |
| PNL | causal-learn | up to 1,470x | true on more pairs than causal-learn's |

The [0.4.0 release card](../docs/benchmarks/0.4.0.md) reports every measurement, including the
sizes, machine, and settings.

## Other highlights

- **Precise p-values.** From 0.16.0, a p-value below about 1e-16 no longer rounds to 0. When both
  p-values used to read 0, the smaller statistic decided; now the larger p-value decides. Every
  method that tests with HSIC computes its p-values this way, including RCD, BottomUpParceLiNGAM,
  GIN, and the KCI test without conditions.
- **ANM in any units.** ANM standardizes each column first, so its answer does not depend on either
  variable's units.

For details, read the [changelog](../docs/changelog.md).

## Real pairs

From 0.18.0, `andrey.data.load_dataset("tuebingen")` loads the Tübingen cause-effect pairs (Mooij et
al., 2016), a collection of real measurements whose direction is known. It holds 108 pairs, each a
dataset, the graph of its reference direction, and its `cause` and `effect` columns. In 102 pairs,
the cause and the effect are one column each, and the graph has two nodes. Six pairs hold a block of
several columns as the cause or the effect and are marked `multivariate`; the graph of such a pair
has an arc from each cause column to each effect column. Each pair also has a weight: the pairs from
one source share weights that sum to 1, so an average over the collection counts each source once.
The collection states no license, so Andrey downloads it from its original server on first use and
checks the file.
